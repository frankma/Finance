The goal

Win chips. Each hand you either make the best five-card hand at the showdown, or bet so that everyone else folds before it. Each player has two private hole cards; five shared board cards come face up in the middle. Your hand is the best five of your two and the board's five, in any mix.

Hand rankings

Best first. Within a category, higher cards win: a pair of kings beats a pair of queens, and with the same pair the highest other card, the kicker, decides. Suits never rank.

The button and the blinds

The button (D) marks the dealer's seat and moves one seat left each hand. The two seats after it post forced bets before any cards: the small blind (SB) and the big blind (BB), here 1 and 2 chips. The blinds give everyone something to win from the start.

Heads-up, with only two players, the button posts the small blind.

The four betting rounds

  1. Preflop: everyone gets two hole cards. Betting starts left of the big blind.
  2. Flop: three board cards. Betting starts with the first player still in, left of the button.
  3. Turn: a fourth board card, then betting.
  4. River: the fifth board card, then the last betting round.
A full board after the river.

A round ends when everyone still in has acted and put in the same amount, or is all in.

Your moves

MoveKeyWhen
FoldFGive up the hand and whatever you have put in.
CheckKPass without betting, when there is nothing to call.
CallKMatch the current bet.
Bet or raiseRPut in more than the current bet; others must call, raise again or fold. The first bet in a round is a bet, any after it a raise.

How much you can raise

  • This is no-limit: you can bet any amount up to everything in front of you.
  • The smallest raise adds at least as much as the last raise did. If the bet is 2 and someone raises to 6, the next raise must be to at least 10.
  • The raise slider offers the minimum, half the pot, the pot and all in.

All in

If you cannot cover a bet, you can call with all your chips. A player all in cannot be forced out, but can only win as much from each other player as they put in. An all-in that adds less than a full raise does not let players who have already acted raise again; they can only call it or fold.

Side pots

When a player is all in for less than the others bet, the chips split into a main pot, which everyone can win, and side pots that only the players who put in more can win.

Example: you are all in for 50; two others bet 120 each. The main pot is 150 (50 from each of you), and you can win it. The side pot is 140 (the other 70 from each of them), and only they can win it.

The showdown

If more than one player is left after the river, the hands are turned over and the best five-card hand wins each pot it is eligible for. Equal hands split the pot; an odd chip goes to the first winner left of the button. If everyone else folds, the last player wins without showing.

On 7♦ 8♠ 9♥ 10♦ J♠ both make the same straight from the board: the pot is split.

Equity

Your equity is your share of the pot if the hand were played to the end many times: the chance you win, with ties counted as part wins. The coach estimates it by dealing the other players hands from the cards you have not seen, thousands of times, and finishing the board each time.

Until someone acts, those hands are random. Once a player raises, bets or calls a bet, the coach favours hands that fit: strong starting hands after a preflop raise, and hands that connect with the board after a bet, more so the bigger the bet. It uses only the public betting, and it never rules a hand out, because players do bluff. Against someone who bets far more than their cards justify, it will be too cautious, so keep your own read of the table.

Pocket aces against one random hand: about 85%.

Pot odds

The equity a call needs to break even: the call divided by the pot it would make.

The pot holds 60 and it costs you 20 to call. The pot would be 80, so you need 20 ÷ 80 = 25% equity. With more, calling makes chips in the long run; with less, folding does.

Outs

Outs are the unseen cards that would improve your hand. A quick estimate, the rule of 2 and 4: on the flop, outs × 4 is roughly your chance to hit by the river; on the turn, outs × 2 is your chance on the river.

A flush draw: 9 hearts left, so about 9 × 4 = 36% by the river (exactly 35%).

The coach also counts cards that only pair the board, which help you a little but help everyone, so its count can run high.

Expected value of a call

The chips a call wins on average, compared with folding: equity × (pot + call) − call.

With 35% equity, a pot of 60 and 20 to call: 0.35 × 80 − 20 = +8 chips. A positive value means the call pays over time, even though you will lose it more often than you win it.

What the coach knows

Your two cards, the board, the pot, the stacks and every bet made. Never another player's cards or the order of the deck: the server never sends them to your browser. The computer players follow the same rule, each from its own cards only.
