The goal

GuanDan is a climbing game for two teams of two. Partners sit opposite each other: here you and your partner against East and West. Each hand, everyone tries to get rid of all their cards by playing them in combinations. The team whose player goes out first wins the hand and climbs levels, from 2 up to A. A team that wins a hand at A wins the match.

The deal and the order of play

Two full packs with all four jokers, 108 cards, are dealt out: 27 to each player. Play passes from you to East, to your partner, to West and back to you. Who leads the first hand is drawn at random; after that, tribute decides.

The order of the cards

From low to high: 2, 3, 4 … 10, J, Q, K, A, then the level rank, then the black joker, then the red joker. The level rank is the level being played: at level 7, the 7s rank above the aces. Suits do not rank.

At level 7, low to high: K, A, the level 7, black joker, red joker.

The level and wild cards

Both teams start at level 2, and each hand is played at the level of the team that won the last one. The two hearts of the level rank are wild: each can stand in for any card except a joker. At level 2 the wilds are the two 2♥; at level 7 the two 7♥. They are outlined in your hand.

At level 2, the wild 2♥ makes three 9s.

Some selections read more than one way with a wild: 2♥ 5♠ 6♠ 7♠ 8♠ can be a straight flush or a straight, to 8 or to 9. The table then asks which you mean.

In straights, tubes and plates the cards keep their natural order: the level rank sits in its usual place, not above the ace.

Bombs

Bombs beat every ordinary combination, whatever was led, and only a bigger bomb beats a bomb. From weakest to strongest:

BombExample
Four of a kind
Five of a kind

Bombs of the same size rank by their cards, and a bigger bomb always beats a smaller one: any five of a kind beats any four of a kind.

Playing a trick

  1. The leader plays any combination.
  2. Each player in turn either beats it, with the same kind of combination of the same size and higher rank, or with a bomb, or passes.
  3. Passing does not stop you playing later in the same trick.
  4. When everyone else still holding cards passes in a row, the last player to play wins the trick and leads the next one.

Going out and catching the wind

When a player plays their last card they go out, and the others play on. If the player who goes out wins that trick, their partner leads the next one: catching the wind. If the partner is out too, the next player with cards leads.

The hand ends as soon as both players of one team are out.

Scoring: climbing levels

The team of the first player out wins the hand. How far it climbs depends on where the partner finished:

The next hand is played at the winning team's new level.

Tribute

Before every hand after the first, the losers pay for the last hand:

  • The last player out gives their highest card, never a wild, to the first player out.
  • After a double win both losers pay; the bigger card goes to the first player out and the other to the second.
  • Each player who receives tribute hands one card back: a 10 or lower if they have one, never a wild or a joker.
  • The player who paid the bigger tribute leads the hand.
  • If the players who must pay hold both red jokers between them, the tribute is resisted: nobody pays, and the first player out of the last hand leads.

When you receive tribute, the table shows what was paid and outlines the cards you may hand back.

Winning the match

Levels never go past A. A team at A wins the match by winning a hand played at its level with its partner finishing 2nd or 3rd. A team that tries at A and fails three times drops back to level 2.

Playing well

  • Plan the whole hand. Group your cards into as few plays as you can. The coach scores every play by how easy it leaves the rest of your hand to play out.
  • Keep bombs back until an opponent is close to going out, or a bomb wins you the lead and the rest of your hand follows.
  • Do not beat your partner when they are winning a trick. Help them out instead: lead a low single or pair when they hold one or two cards.
  • Spend wilds carefully. They are most useful to finish or to make a bomb.
  • Count the big cards. The table tracks the jokers, wilds, level cards and aces you have not seen, so you know what can still beat you.

What the coach knows

Your hand, the trick to beat, how many cards each player holds, every card played and every pass. Never your partner's or your opponents' cards: the server never sends them to your browser. The computer players, your partner included, follow the same rule, each from its own hand only.
